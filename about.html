Who we are

Necessity Robotics is a robotics company in Bhilai, Chhattisgarh, India. Two founders run it:

  • Sumedh, founder. Author of our two technical papers: on xclean, and on our depth model, nrs-large. Neither paper is released yet. A summary of the xclean paper is on our Paper page.
  • Karan, co-founder. B.Tech in computer science.

Our first product is live SLAM: software that tells a robot where it is. A lost robot cannot work, so we built this first.

We are at an early stage. We are raising our first round.

What works today

We have two products. Both are software, and both are free under Apache-2.0, also for commercial use. No robot is built yet.

Live SLAM xclean Tells a robot where it is, from a stereo camera. It uses no satellite signal (GPS). 20.4 ms from the arrival of an image to its position (median, on one desktop PC with an NVIDIA RTX 5090). Tested on 216 real-time replays of 72 recorded routes: an indoor drone, cars, a rover, a farm robot and our own OAK-D-Lite camera. We score only the positions it gives live. It is not more accurate than the two systems we compared it with. Released, open source. Live on a camera so far only on a tripod, standing still (OAK-D-Lite). We test moving routes as real-time replays. Zero-shot ML Depth model How far away each point is, from two camera photos. No retraining for a new calibrated stereo camera (pinhole lenses). We trained it from scratch, in 92 hours on one graphics card, on a public set of computer-made images (WMGStereo-150k). It passed 4 of 4 pass marks that we set before training, on four public sets of real photos it never saw. On one other set it scored worse than the published result for the same design. The network design (DLNR) and the training recipe are from other teams. The trained weights are ours. It is slow (0.28 s per photo pair on one RTX 5090), so we use it after recording, not live. nrs-large: Free download. We publish the weights and the code to run it.

Our map builder, xclean-maps, is open source too. Its released version (0.1.0) uses a classic depth method. Our 3-D places use our depth model, through a newer version that is not released yet.

Our goal

Pass the physical Turing test.

A robot passes it when you cannot tell its work from a person's. We see a future of physical AI: robots that do real tasks as well as people, then better.

Good software is not enough for that. A robot that passes the test needs energy, semiconductors, hardware and software that work as one. So we aim to build the complete robotics ecosystem ourselves, in five layers:

  • Energy: the power a robot runs on. Not built yet.
  • Semiconductors: the chips a robot thinks with. Not built yet.
  • Hardware: the body, with its motors, joints and hands. Not built yet.
  • Software: what lets a robot see, know where it is and act. The first part works today.
  • Product: the finished robot, made from the other four layers. Not built yet.

What we are building

These are aims. None of them is finished. Only the first part of one aim works today.

  • Dexterous manipulation: robot hands that grasp, hold and use real objects with the skill of a person.
  • Navigation in any environment: robots that find their way and move with ease indoors, outdoors and in places that are too dangerous for people. The first part works today: xclean, our live SLAM, tells a robot where it is.
  • Every form factor: the same skills in every type of robot body: robot arms, wheeled robots, legged robots and humanoids.
  • Surveillance for defence: systems that watch from the air and from the ground, and find their way where satellite signals are weak or jammed.

How we work

  • We set pass marks before the test. We write them down before a run or a training starts.
  • We publish every official result of our released work: the failed tests too, and the places where other systems do better.
  • We name the work of others: network designs, datasets and software.
  • Aims stay aims. We never show unfinished work as done.
