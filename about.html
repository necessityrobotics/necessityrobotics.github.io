What works today

We have xclean, our live SLAM, two depth models (nrs-large and nrs-edge) and a map builder, xclean-maps. All are software, and all are free under Apache-2.0, also for commercial use. No working robot is built yet.

Live SLAM xclean Tells a robot where it is, from a stereo camera. It uses no satellite signal (GPS). 20.4 ms from the arrival of an image to its position (median, on one desktop PC with an NVIDIA RTX 5090). Tested on 216 real-time replays of 72 recorded routes: an indoor drone, cars, a rover, a farm robot and our own OAK-D-Lite camera. We score only the positions it gives live. It is not more accurate than the two systems we compared it with (ORB-SLAM3 and OKVIS2-X): on the 38 routes that we kept out of development, against ORB-SLAM3 (run offline, untuned), it was better on 10 (on 3 of them the ORB-SLAM3 runs had a problem), equal on 2 and worse on 26. Released, open source. One pip command to install. Every route and every loss is on our Results page. Zero-shot ML Depth model How far away each point is, from two camera photos. No retraining for a new calibrated stereo camera (pinhole lenses). We trained it from scratch, in 92 hours on one graphics card, on a public set of computer-made images (WMGStereo-150k). It passed 4 of 4 pass marks that we set before training, on four public sets of real photos it never saw. On one other set it scored worse than the published result for the same design. The network design (DLNR) and the training recipe are from other teams. The trained weights are ours. It is slow (0.28 s per photo pair on one RTX 5090), so we use it after recording, not live. nrs-large: Free download. We publish the weights and the code to run it. New: nrs-edge, a small model (6 million parameters), also a free download.

Our map builder, xclean-maps, is open source too. Its released version (0.1.0) uses a classic depth method. Our 3-D places use our depth model, through a newer version that is not released yet.

Our goal

Pass the physical Turing test.

A robot passes it when you cannot tell its work from a person's. We see a future of physical AI: robots that do real tasks as well as people, then better.

Our plan, in order

  • NowSoftware. What lets a robot know where it is, and how far away things are. Released: xclean, nrs-large, nrs-edge, and our map builder xclean-maps.
  • NextOur first robot. Four wheels and two arms, running our software. It will work outdoors, in the real world, from its first step: first our software on the moving robot, then one arm, then both arms on a street. Milestone 1, the goal of our first round: it will pick up litter on a street in India. Milestone 2, our mission, after milestone 1: the same work in the mountains of north India. We will publish the result of each step. Not built yet. Our first round will pay for the robot and its steps to milestone 1: see For investors.
  • LaterHands and more bodies. Robot hands with the skill of a person, on wheeled robots, legged robots and humanoids. Not started.
  • LaterOur own stack. Our own actuators and control boards, our own chips for the computing inside the robot, and renewable power for our work. Not started, except a first arm prototype. The reason for each layer is below.

Why we aim to build the whole stack

Good software is not enough. A robot that passes the test needs energy, semiconductors, hardware and software that work as one. So we aim to build each layer ourselves, in time. Of the three layers below, only hardware has a first step.

  • Hardware: our own actuators and control boards. Then the cost, the supply and the design of a robot would not depend on other companies. First step: an arm prototype of our own design, with bought actuators. One joint turns under power. See Hardware so far. Our own actuators and boards are not started.
  • Semiconductors: our own chips for inference, the computing inside the robot. Training stays on graphics cards that we buy: we do not plan to compete there.
  • Energy: renewable power for our work and for our model training. The energy and water use of data centres is a known problem. We want to address it in our own work from the start.

What we are building

These are our aims for later. None of them is finished. Only the first part of one aim works today.

  • Dexterous manipulation: robot hands that grasp, hold and use real objects with the skill of a person.
  • Navigation in any environment: robots that find their way and move with ease indoors, outdoors, where there is no satellite signal, and in places that are too dangerous for people. The first part works today: xclean, our live SLAM, tells a robot where it is.
  • Every form factor: the same skills in every type of robot body: robot arms, wheeled robots, legged robots and humanoids.

How we work

  • We set pass marks before the test. We write them down before a run or a training starts.
  • We publish every official result of our released work: the failed tests too, and the places where other systems do better.
  • We name the work of others: network designs, datasets and software.
  • Aims stay aims. We never show unfinished work as done.
