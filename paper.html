Summary

Robots act on the pose their state estimator gives them at each frame. We present xclean, a stereo visual-inertial SLAM system whose GPU code is written in CuPy, with no learned networks in its SLAM pipeline, and we score it on exactly what it publishes live: runs paced at the sensors' real rate, every frame's pose as published, rules fixed before the runs. On a final board of 72 routes and 216 live runs, its median frame-to-pose latency is 20.4 ms (p95 26.4 ms) on one desktop GPU, and no run was lost on the 38 routes it had not run before. It is not more accurate than the systems we compared it with: against offline ORB-SLAM3 on those 38 routes it wins 10, ties 2 and loses 26. We report every loss.

This page is a summary. The full paper is being revised to the final board; until it is released, the numbers here and on the results page are the reference.

Why score the live output

A humanoid handing someone a cup, or a mobile robot opening a door, acts on its estimate of where it is at the moment it acts. That estimate sees each frame once, must answer before the next frame arrives, and can never revise an answer it already gave. Published SLAM accuracy is usually measured on the trajectory saved at the end of the run, after global optimisation has revised every pose with hindsight, and often with the data fed slower than real time. That number can be far from what the robot received.

Method, in brief

  • Front end. Stereo feature tracking on the GPU, with sparse stereo matching for depth.
  • Association by map matching. Features are matched against the local map, not only the previous frame.
  • Estimator. A tightly coupled visual-inertial sliding-window solve on the GPU; each frame's pose comes from a solve that contains that frame.
  • IMU. Initialisation, an online trust watchdog and an online camera-IMU clock-offset estimate.
  • Full SLAM. Loop closure with a global correction applied live, relocalisation, and map reuse.
  • One configuration. One frozen build ran every route on the board.
  • Maps. 3-D Gaussian-splat maps are built afterwards from the images and xclean's live poses (maps).

Live evaluation protocol

  • Harness. Frames and IMU samples are released at their recorded timestamps; the system never sees data newer than what was released; every published pose is recorded by a separate process.
  • Live scorer. It scores the pose stream as published, every frame, with no run excluded.
  • Bars fixed in advance. Latency, drops, continuity, robustness, accuracy and resources, each with a written pass rule.
  • Kill tests and unseen routes. Each design step was decided by a pre-registered test. The final board's headline uses 38 routes xclean had never been run on.
  • Baselines. ORB-SLAM3 offline and untuned (5 runs per route) and OKVIS2-X causal and unpaced (1 run per route), run separately; none of their code is part of xclean.

Limitations

  • Not more accurate than ORB-SLAM3 or OKVIS2-X on our board.
  • The engine was frozen with kill test K14 failed on two routes (madmax_E0, kitti_02); the board ran on its twin build with identical engine code.
  • Robustness: on 2 of the 38 unseen routes a replay attempt crashed or hung, and 4 failed the unsupported-frames check.
  • One machine: all timings come from one desktop with an RTX 5090. The embedded port is not done.
  • Some datasets' ground truth has known faults (MADMAX heading, Rosario position-only, 4Seasons scale); these affect every system scored.
  • Our own OAK-D-Lite routes have no ground truth: latency, drops and visual inspection only.
