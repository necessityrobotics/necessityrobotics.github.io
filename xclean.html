xclean

Real-time stereo visual-inertial SLAM, built in India. Python, with GPU code written in CuPy and CPU hot paths in Numba: no C++ build step, no ROS. It runs natively on Windows 11 and is built for a Luxonis OAK-D-Lite stereo camera.

What it does

xclean estimates the pose of a stereo camera at every frame, while it moves. Every pose is published live from a solve that contains that frame, and loop corrections are applied live.

xclean is a stereo visual-inertial SLAM system: it uses the IMU while a built-in consistency check accepts it, and otherwise continues as stereo visual odometry with loop closure. In the final board the IMU stayed in use to the end on 50 of 216 runs (mostly EuRoC and 4Seasons). On MADMAX, Rosario and our own OAK-D-Lite recordings the check dropped or refused it. KITTI has no IMU. Per dataset.

  • Front end on the GPU: stereo feature tracking.
  • Inertial: IMU preintegration, with an online consistency check (it can drop or refuse the IMU) and an online camera-IMU clock-offset estimate.
  • Back end on the GPU: a sliding-window factor graph.
  • Full SLAM: loop closure, relocalisation and map reuse.
  • No learned networks in the SLAM pipeline.

"Written in CuPy" does not mean "pure Python": CuPy compiles our CUDA C source at first use (NVRTC), and the CPU hot paths are compiled by Numba.

Measured on one desktop (RTX 5090) with its normal background load, host-side from frame arrival to the loop-corrected pose, in our development environment. The 38 routes were held out from xclean's development; 10 of them were run in a set-aside first launch whose results were never read. Robustness caveat: on 2 of those 38 routes a replay attempt crashed or hung, and the unsupported-frames check passed on 9 routes, failed on 4 and is pending on 25 (all MADMAX, whose ground truth has known faults). The engine was frozen with one of its pre-registered tests (K14) failed on two routes and passed on a third (kitti_09) only through its no-harm clause; details. Source: the final board of 2026-10-30 (data/board.json on this site).

Install and run

Install and run instructions will be published with the code. What you will need: an NVIDIA GPU and driver. The full requirements come with the release.

Known issue (being fixed before release)

In the current install environment the live engine (paced and lock-step, which use the GPU front end on an NVIDIA machine) stops at a start-up self-check: its GPU and CPU results for small 3×3 solves do not match. The final board ran in our development environment, which is a different one. We will publish the install route once it reproduces the engine.
