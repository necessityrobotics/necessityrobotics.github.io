Good to know

Why black and white?

The 5 places from India are black and white, because our camera's two eyes see no colour. The 3 field places from Argentina are in colour.

Why does it look soft?

A computer built it from the photos. No person touched it up.

Where it is not perfect

Where it is not perfect

No place here is perfect. For every place, the camera positions come from our software alone, in a replay of the recording at real speed. No outside measurement corrected them. Where a position was a little off, a surface can look soft or doubled. Each place says where.

For engineers: how these places are made, and the files to download

Each 3-D place is a 3-D Gaussian-splat map. Each map is reconstructed from a route's images and xclean's own live camera poses, not ground truth. Where the poses are wrong, the map shows it. Each map says where.

Only maps from data we may share are published here: our own OAK-D-Lite recordings and the Rosario Dataset (CC BY 4.0). Routes from datasets with non-commercial or unstated licences appear on the results page as numbers only. The poses of each map are the median run of the three final-board runs (by ATE on routes with ground truth; on our OAK routes, the run whose end position is closest to the other two). Map facts are read from each map's own files (attribution, manifest, trajectory, point cloud); path length is the sum of xclean's keyframe-to-keyframe distances. The board numbers shown come from an engine frozen with its last pre-registered kill test (K14) failed on two routes; details.
